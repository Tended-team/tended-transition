# PRODUCT.md — Tended Transition

> Living brief for the Tended leadership transition site. Items marked **[confirm]**
> are working assumptions Joe should verify or correct.

## Register

**brand** — this site *is* the message. It's a public-facing communication piece, not
an app. Design carries trust, clarity, and warmth.

## Context: two brands

- **Tended** — in-home elder-care platform and holding company (Tended Holdings). The
  **go-forward brand**. Purple + gold identity, DM Serif Display. Brand guide lives in
  Notion; asset kit in SharePoint.
- **ApexCare** — Northern California in-home care provider since 1992 (offices across
  Sacramento, Stockton, Vacaville, Concord, Napa; ~10 counties). The **heritage brand**
  joining Tended. Blue/teal/green identity, Montserrat.

This site communicates the **ApexCare → Tended transition** to the ApexCare community.

## Product purpose

A leadership entry / transition plan, modeled structurally on a "first 100 days" entry
plan. It tells the community how the new leadership will **Connect, Discover, and
Deliver** through the transition: who they'll listen to, what they'll focus on, the
timeline of public updates, and the commitments they're making. **[confirm]** whether
the framing is a new leader's entry plan, a rebrand announcement, or both.

## Users

- **Primary:** ApexCare families, clients, and caregivers affected by the move to
  Tended. Often older or not especially technical; reading on a phone. They want
  reassurance that care continues uninterrupted and to know what changes for them.
- **Secondary:** ApexCare office staff, and external readers forming a first impression.

Emotional target: calm confidence and continuity of care. Not corporate hype, not
crisis. "Your care continues, you're in steady hands, here's exactly what happens next,
and here's how to be heard."

## Brand

- **Name meaning:** *Tended* — care, cultivation, stewardship, patient growth. The brand
  should feel human and unhurried, the opposite of move-fast disruption. **[confirm]**
- **Voice:** first-person, direct, plainspoken, honest. Short declarative sentences.
  Promises framed as "I will…". No jargon, no hype.
- **Anti-references:** generic SaaS landing pages; school-district / government template
  look; stock "hero metric + three feature cards" layouts.

## Strategic principles

1. Structure mirrors the reference (hero → letter → focus areas → 3-stage process →
   topics → timeline → commitments → events → CTA), branding does not.
2. Every claim is concrete and dated. Vagueness reads as evasion here.
3. One clear action per section max. The whole page drives toward "share your input."
